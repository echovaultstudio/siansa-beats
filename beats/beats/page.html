          Genres

          Each song's genres, heard from its sound. The Siansa app's genre list, genre pages and genre radios use these.

          Loading the genres…
          How it works

          A model hears each song and scores it against 400 styles (the Discogs list). A song is in every style it scores 0.15 or more, and always in its best one. The genre tags in your files are shown beside for comparison and play no part.

          Descriptions

          Each genre's page in the app opens with a short description from Wikipedia. They're fetched in the background and kept, so the app shows them at once.

          How these are made

          Siansa Beats looks at when you play what. The week is cut in ten parts: early morning, morning, afternoon, evening and night, on weekdays and at the weekend. The songs you play in a part fall into a few kinds that sound alike, and each kind is a mix: some of your own songs, and around them songs that sound like them and that you haven't played for a while. A recent listen counts for more, and so does one from this time of year in earlier years.

          The sun is worked out from the date and your time zone's city: no weather service is asked. In the dark the calmer mixes come first. A mix is named by its sound, never by the weather, and stays the same all day.

          With too little listening in a part, it borrows from that time on other days, then from all your listening, then from your library. A song you skip before it counts as a listen isn't one. The app tells Siansa Beats about it (Siansa Beats 0.64 and an app that sends skips): each skip halves what that song's listens count for, a skipped song doesn't come back as a new find for a while, and a skip fades as a listen does. Made around › Songs you skip count for less switches this off.

          The next part of the day's mixes are made ten minutes before it starts, so the app finds them ready. With an AI service set up in Setup, each mix of a kind of music gets a name and a short line from it.

          Mix settings

          Siansa Beats makes your mixes by itself, from what you play, the time of day, the season and the light outside. Change only what you want.

            How it works

            Essentia's trained models read what Siansa Beats already keeps of each song's sound. No song is heard again. A song has a mood when its share is half or more.                                 Listeners

                                Every name the app scrobbles as, or a file was brought in for, has a history of its own. Two that are one person's can be joined: a listen both have is kept once.

                                  Memory

                                  Your listens are read into memory to work the numbers out fast, and let go when Listening hasn't been opened for a while. Nothing is lost either way: they stay in the database.

                                  Bring in a history

                                  An export of Koito, of Last.fm (the JSON or CSV of an export tool) or of ListenBrainz. A listen that's here already is kept once, so a file can be brought in again.

                                  On to Last.fm

                                  Your own Last.fm API account (last.fm/api/account/create). The key, secret and sign-in are kept encrypted; the password isn't kept. New listens of this listener are then sent on; turn the app's own Last.fm off, so they aren't sent twice.

                                        Music servers

                                        Navidrome, Emby or Jellyfin. Add as many as you have, or more than one user of the same server. A song two servers both have is analysed once.

                                          Add a server or a user

                                          No password is kept as you type it. Emby's and Jellyfin's is used once to sign in and not kept: only the sign-in the server gives is, encrypted. Navidrome's has to be said to Navidrome each time, so it's kept encrypted. None is ever shown, logged or put in a report, and Siansa Beats only reads from the server.

                                          AI

                                          Mixes get a name from an AI service, like a streaming service's editors would give them. Only a mix's genres, energy, mood and three of its artists are sent: never your listening. Without it, a mix is named by its sound ("Calm Indie Rock").

                                          The Siansa app asks it too: the names and sound searches of its vibes, Today's mix, Flow's words and the mood radios. With it on here, the app needs no AI service of its own.

                                          Access

                                          The app's key, shown this once (only its hash is kept). Copy it into Siansa now:

                                          With a password set, you sign in on a page of its own and stay signed in on this browser. Only a hash of the password and of the key is kept: neither can be read back. Forgotten the password? On the server, run docker exec siansa-beats python -m beats.reset.

                                          Models

                                          What analyses your music, and where it runs.

                                          The models are other people's work, each under its own licence: their authors are credited in the README.

                                          Genre model

                                          Which model hears each song's genres. Both name the same 400 styles.

                                          Choose one before your first analysis. You can change it later, but every song is then heard again.

                                          If you change it later

                                          • What's kept: each song keeps its genres from the model before, still in use, until the new one hears it. Then they're replaced: the two are never kept side by side.
                                          • While it listens: genres are partly old and partly new. Genre pages, the genres in the Siansa app and the mixes made by kind of music change song by song. Beats, DJ blends, loudness, keys, moods, similar music and the search by words don't use the genre model and keep working as they are.
                                          • After: the old model's genres are gone. Going back means hearing every song again.

                                          Vocals for DJ blends

                                          Beats pulls the voice out of the first and last 1½ minutes of each song and marks, bar by bar, where someone sings. Siansa's DJ blends use it so two vocals are never heard at the same time. Without it, DJ blends still work: they just don't know where the singing is.

                                          Slow. Pulling the voice out takes much longer than the rest of the analysis, most of all without a GPU: for a big library it can take days. It only starts a song when nothing else is being analysed, but a song added meanwhile is analysed alongside it, more slowly. Leave it off if you don't use DJ blends.

                                          Ticked: only songs the voice model says are sung are separated; the rest count as instrumental. Faster, but a song with only a little singing can be missed. Unticked: every song is separated.

                                          Only vocals pauses the rest of the analysis and finds the vocals now.
